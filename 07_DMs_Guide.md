# Chapter 7: Dungeon Master's Guide

A Wayfinder campaign should feel like routes opening, rivals responding, nations adjusting, and legends forming. The DM's role is not to restrict the Wayfinder's influence, but to ensure that influence creates new challenges and opportunities.

## Running the Campaign
- **Naval Campaigns:** Track positioning, morale, supplies, and weather. Captain's Techniques create tactical advantages, not automatic victory.
- **Exploration Campaigns:** Exploration techniques should reveal clues and paths, but they do not eliminate the discovery process. Discovering a new route creates story (e.g., rival explorers, ancient guardians).
- **Political Campaigns:** First Captain techniques create influence and reputation, not mind control. The captain can gain audiences or secure trade agreements, but kings do not automatically surrender their thrones.
- **Fleet Warfare:** Fleet conflicts are strategic encounters. Ask: Who has superior intelligence? Who controls positioning? What are the objectives? 

## Handling Consequences
Every major achievement creates reactions.
- **Immediate Effect:** What changes now?
- **Local Response:** Who notices? (e.g., local pirates, merchants).
- **Regional Response:** Who reacts? (e.g., Crimson Dominion investigates, Drow launch a raid).
- **Long-Term Consequence:** How does the world change? (e.g., a new era of exploration begins).

### Tracking the World
- **Captain Reputation:** Fame, fear, and political influence.
- **Maritime Relations:** Allies, enemies, and neutral factions.
- **Known Routes:** Safe paths, dangers, and discoveries.

## Exploit Prevention & Rules Clarifications
To preserve the campaign sandbox and prevent the Wayfinder from bypassing all gameplay:

1. **Fleet + First Captain Interaction:** 
   - *Rule:* Fleet techniques affect tactical command during active operations. First Captain techniques affect strategic influence. A creature can benefit from only **one** Fleet technique command effect at a time.
2. **Mystic/Hunter Information Limits:**
   - *Rule:* Information gained through techniques provides direction, clues, and probabilities. It does not automatically reveal all hidden creatures, secret motives, or bypass investigation. Perfect information destroys mystery.
3. **Corsair Siege Limits:**
   - *Rule:* Siege-related techniques create openings and tactical advantages. They do not automatically destroy structures, ships, or magical defenses. You create a breach; the fortress does not instantly collapse.
4. **Legendary World Effects:**
   - *Rule:* Permanent world-changing effects (e.g., discovering impossible routes, establishing sovereign domains) require time, resources, and narrative consequences. The captain can influence history, but they do not own it.
5. **Passive & Action Economy Limits:**
   - *Rule:* Bonuses from Captain's Techniques follow normal D&D 3.5 stacking rules. A Wayfinder may use only **one** Immediate-Action Captain's Technique per round.

## Final DM Principle
*The captain may change the world, but the world always responds. A Wayfinder's greatest victories should not close the campaign; they should create the next chapter.*
