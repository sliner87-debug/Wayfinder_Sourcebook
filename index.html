Wayfinder Corsair Campaign Setting & Rulebook

Chapter 1: Campaign Framework

The World: The Shattered Expanse

Nearly 800 years ago came The Sundering Tide. No one knows exactly what happened—whether the gods went to war, an elder civilization awakened something beneath the crust, or the oceans became sentient. The world shattered into tens of thousands of islands. New islands emerge, entire nations drift, and maps are never permanent.

Only about 35% of the world’s oceans have reliable charts. The rest is The Unwritten Sea.

Core Campaign Blend

This campaign is an exploration sandbox blending “Age of Sail” themes with “High Fantasy” elements: - The Uncharted Ocean (40%): The world is not fully known. Maps are wrong. The primary gameplay loop is Rumor → Expedition → Discovery → Map → Route → Trade → Conflict. - The Great Magical Trade Network (35%): The economic engine. Discoveries matter because new resources (e.g., Moon Coral) create trade opportunities, drawing merchants, pirates, and governments. - The Pirate Renaissance (20%): Pirates are the first people willing to go where governments cannot. The era creates factions—traditional raiders, pirate nobility, independent explorers. - The Underdark Ocean (15%): A massive subterranean ecosystem containing ancient cities, drowned kingdoms, and alien ecosystems like the Aboleth Archives and Drow courts. - The Lost Civilization Mystery (100% Underlying Engine): Why are oceans unexplored? Because someone made them unexplorable. Everything connects back to The First Captains.

The First Captains

The original explorers. The first people to bond with ships and spirits. They mastered the connection between Arcane, Psionics, Spirits, and the Ocean. At some point in history, they vanished.

[!NOTE] Level 20 Capstone Quest: Upon reaching level 20, a Wayfinder is offered a legendary quest to “Restore the First Captains.” This world-altering choice asks the player to restore, replace, hide, or release the greatest explorers in history.

The Four Magic Systems

Magic enhances naval warfare rather than replacing it. Ships remain the kings of warfare. 1. Arcane (The Pact): Traditional magic and warlock pacts. Power borrowed from sea devils, leviathans, living storms, or star beings. “I borrowed power from something older than the world.” 2. Psionics (The Mind): Power originating from consciousness itself. Some surface kingdoms fear it; the greatest psionic cities lie beneath the earth. “I discovered that my own consciousness is a weapon.” 3. Spirit (The Tide): Primal sea magic. Not worship, but alignment with the ocean’s natural order. “I learned the hidden laws of the sea itself.” 4. Deep Magic: Predates all three. It changes gravity, distance, probability, and time. It is the source of many impossible places and artifacts.

Major Factions

The ocean is a deeply political ecosystem: - The Free Captains Alliance (Blackwake Harbor): A pirate frontier valuing exploration and independence. - The Grand Merchant Consortium (Port Meridian): A neutral trade power that doesn’t own the city, just everything inside it. - The Crimson Dominion (The Crimson Citadel): A wealthy arcane empire built on slavery, magical superiority, and the exploitation of conquered peoples. They view Wayfinders and new routes as economic threats. - The Drowned Silk Houses (Nymorath Deep Harbor): An ancient Drow Underdark Ocean civilization. They raid the surface for resources, ancient claims, and political warfare. They remember the First Captains. - The Spirit Keepers (The Last Harbor): A hidden sanctuary of guardians preserving old traditions, Navigator Spirits, and First Captain lore. - Independent Salvagers (Salvager’s Haven): A frontier port of wreck hunters, explorers, and forgotten discoveries.

Chapter 2: Starting Origins

Every Wayfinder campaign begins with a shipwreck. The party does not start with a ship; they start with survival.

The Tide’s Choice

There are two opening methods to determine where and how the players begin their adventure.

Method A: The Captain’s Choice

“I chose where I wanted to go. The sea decided how I arrived.”

The player chooses their Home Port and starting faction environment. The sea (via random dice rolls or GM selection) determines: - Shipwreck Cause - Rescue Vessel - Rescue Circumstances - Initial Reputation

Method B: The Sea’s Choice

“I surrendered my destination, but I wrote my legend.”

The player rolls randomly for their Home Port, letting fate decide their starting faction, local allies, and initial enemies. In return, the player chooses: - Shipwreck Cause - Rescue Vessel - Arrival Circumstance

Shipwreck Causes

The reason the ship went down shapes the player’s initial mystery and backstory. Causes include: - Leviathan attack - Magical storm / Psionic distortion - Drow naval assault / Crimson Dominion experiment - Pirate battle / Mutiny / Betrayal - Ancient defense system activated - Underdark Ocean current breach / Planar ocean anomaly - Ship vanished and reappeared damaged

Rescue Vessels & Circumstances

The rescuers do not necessarily know they are rescuing someone important. The ship that pulls you from the water determines your first relationship and faction tie.

Rescuer Options

  • Blackwake Connection: A pirate exploration vessel hunting for treasure.
  • Port Meridian Connection: A merchant expedition charting routes.
  • Crimson Dominion Connection: A slaver/arcane vessel that recognizes something unusual about your survival.
  • Drow Connection: Drow scouts drifting near an Underdark Ocean route.
  • Spirit Keeper Connection: A vessel guided by spirits, deliberately looking for you.
  • Salvager Connection: Wreck hunters where you are just another survivor.

Arrival Circumstances

How you are treated on the rescue ship heavily influences your arrival at the Home Port: - Honored Guest: Treated well. - Prisoner: Rescued, but questioned and held under suspicion. - Debt Owed: Saved, but expecting repayment. - Mystery Passenger: The crew does not understand why you survived. - Prophecy/Recognition: Someone on the ship knows exactly what you are.

Chapter 3: Races & Transformations

The base race of this setting is the Tideborn Pixie, but players do not stay “just” pixies. As they explore the oceans, they undergo the Fey Transformation System, accumulating templates that reflect the magic, ruins, and pacts they encounter.

Base Race: Tideborn Pixie

“A Pixie born from ocean currents, ancient routes, and the forgotten magic between shores.”

Type: Fey (Small) Speed: 20 ft. Land / 60 ft. Fly (Good) / 30 ft. Swim (Good) Ability Adjustments: Str -4, Dex +8, Int +6, Wis +4, Cha +8

Racial Traits: - Tideborn Amphibious Nature: Ignores underwater movement penalties and natural ocean terrain penalties. Can breathe underwater 1 hr/HD/day (permanent at 5 HD). - Fey Sight: Low-light vision. Spirit Sight: Detect Fey/Spirits/Magical Residue at will (30 ft. range). - Fey Magic: Original 3.0 Pixie Spell-Like Abilities. - Polymorph: The original 3.0 Pixie Polymorph ability (self only). Cannot gain spell-like, supernatural, or extraordinary abilities from the new form. - Psionic Spark: Base Power Points = 1/2 Character Level + Cha Mod (min 1). Start with one power: Detect Psionics, Far Hand, or Minor Metamorphosis.

Players may acquire transformations during their journeys. These are not simply chosen from a menu; they are earned or inflicted by the world.

Chapter 4: The Wayfinder Class

“A Wayfinder is not a swashbuckler with magic, a warlock with a boat, or a psion with a sword. The Wayfinder is the rediscovery of an ancient First Captain tradition where Arcane magic shapes reality, Psionics shapes perception, and Spirit magic shapes connection. At low levels, these are three separate disciplines. At high levels, the Wayfinder becomes someone who does not simply navigate the world—they alter the routes the world allows.”

Class Chassis

Hit Die: d8 Alignment: Any Class Skills: 8 + Int modifier per level. (Balance, Bluff, Concentration, Craft (Ships), Decipher Script, Diplomacy, Gather Information, Handle Animal, Intimidate, Knowledge (Arcana/Geography/History/Local/Nature/Psionics/Religion), Profession (Sailor), Search, Sense Motive, Spellcraft, Spot, Survival, Swim, UMD, UPD). Base Attack Bonus: Medium (+15 at Level 20). Saving Throws: Good Reflex and Will, Poor Fortitude. Weapon & Armor Proficiency: Simple weapons, martial light weapons, rapier, cutlass, hand crossbow, shortbow, longbow, firearms. Light armor (Medium at level 5), shields (except tower).

The Threefold Magic Systems

The Wayfinder progresses in three magical paths simultaneously. 1. The Pact (Arcane): Warlock chassis. Uses Charisma. Eldritch Armament (can manifest Eldritch Blast through a weapon, harpoon, pistol, or standard blast) and Invocations. 2. The Mind (Psionic): Psion chassis (half-progression, max 6th-level powers). Uses Charisma. Gains Power Points and discovers powers. 3. The Tide (Spirit): Custom Spirit Shaman+ chassis. Full 1-9 spell progression but spells must be discovered/learned via the Captain’s Log instead of prepared from a deity.

Lore-Caster (The Fusion Engine)

Instead of preparing spells, the Wayfinder uncovers Wayfinding Lore—an accumulated understanding of the world. - Level 7 (Arcane-Psionic Fusion): Combine a spell and a power into one action (e.g., Fireball + Telekinesis = Moving Flame Sphere). - Level 10 (Threefold Awakening - Triune Casting): 1/day, combine a Spell, a Power, and a Spirit Ability to open new paths to unseen locations. - Level 17 (Reality Navigation): You do not teleport; you discover a path through solid fog, magical barriers, or impassable storms. - Level 20 (Creator of Paths): Create permanent routes, impossible trade lanes, and open new ocean passages.

The Navigator Spirit

At level 1, the Wayfinder awakens a personal spirit companion—the “mirror” of the captain. 1. Origin Seed: Choose the spirit’s foundational nature (e.g., Ancient Navigator, Stormborn, Abyssal, Leviathan, Ancestor Captain, Ghost Fleet). 2. Seed Benefit: A deliberate expression of the spirit (e.g., Storm Sense, Lost Routes, Deep Survival). 3. Random Traits: Roll 2d100 for an Innate Trait and an Emergent Trait (e.g., Ancient Memory, Hates Calm Waters, Echo of Darkness). 4. Evolution: The spirit evolves at levels 5, 10, 15, and 20 based on the captain’s actions (e.g., Guardian Aspect, Sage Aspect, Dominator Aspect, Pathfinder Aspect). At level 20, it becomes the embodiment of your legend.

The Living Ship Bond

The ship is your second character. It advances through Vessel Tiers via Wayfinder Level, Chronicle Achievements, and Physical Transformation. - Tier 0 (Mortal Ship): Normal vessel with maintenance costs. - Tier 1 (Claimed Vessel - Lvl 3): Ship recognizes you. You sense its condition. - Tier 2 (Awakened Vessel - Lvl 6): Gains intelligence/personality. Becomes an Arcane, Psionic, or Tide vessel. - Tier 3 (Legendary Vessel - Lvl 10): Threefold Keel. Can install Arcane, Psionic, and Tide upgrades (e.g., Eldritch Cannons, Psychic Helm, Storm Calling). - Tier 4 (Mythic Vessel - Lvl 15): Requires rare materials (dragon bone, deep crystal). Gains traits like Ghost Ship or World Sailor. - Tier 5 (Path-Maker Vessel - Lvl 20): Creates permanent passages through reality. The ship changes the map of the world.

Chapter 5a: Captain’s Techniques (Rules & Framework)

“Every captain leaves behind stories. The First Captains left behind techniques.”

A Captain’s Technique is not a spell, a maneuver, or a psionic power. It is a legendary trick, habit, doctrine, or philosophy that has become supernatural through repetition and authority. The Wayfinder learns 6 Captain’s Techniques over their career (at levels 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18), though class features/milestones may grant more.

Mechanics

  • Save DC Formula: 10 + 1/2 Wayfinder Level + Charisma Modifier.
  • Usage Limits: Unless stated otherwise, they are generally at-will or have specific per-encounter/per-day limits outlined in their text.
  • Action Types: Standard D&D 3.5e actions (Standard, Move, Swift, Immediate, Passive/Special).
    • Global Rule: A Wayfinder may only use one Immediate Action Captain’s Technique per round to prevent reaction bloat.

Categories

Each category has a unique gameplay identity. If a technique solves a problem, the category defines how it solves it. 1. Seamanship: Environmental mastery. Navigation, ship handling, weather, avoiding hazards. “How do I move through the world?” 2. Duelist: Personal combat mastery. Precision, ripostes, officer challenges, exploiting openings. “How do I win this fight?” 3. Corsair: Aggressive raider. Boarding, fear, plunder, breaking defenses (siege). “How do I break the enemy?” (Note: Corsair siege techniques create vulnerabilities, not automatic destruction). 4. Fleet: Tactical commander. Coordination, formations, positioning. “How do we fight as one?” (Note: A creature can benefit from only one Fleet command effect at a time). 5. Hunter: Tracking and pursuit. Reconnaissance, interception, elimination. “How do I find what is hidden?” 6. Mystic: Keeper of sea secrets. Sea magic, omens, supernatural navigation. “What does the world reveal?” (Note: Mystic info provides clues/probabilities, not perfect answers). 7. First Captain: Sovereign commander. Leadership, territory, diplomacy, political authority. “How do I build influence?” (Note: Creates leverage and reputation, not automatic obedience).

The Tiers

  1. Least (Levels 1-5): The Deckhand Becomes an Officer. Focuses on personal skill, small tactical advantages, basic navigation. Scale: Self/1 Ally.
  2. Lesser (Levels 6-11): The Officer Becomes a Captain. Focuses on crew coordination, resource efficiency, and boarding dominance. Scale: Small squad/Ship.
  3. Greater (Levels 12-17): The Captain Becomes an Admiral. Encounter-defining commands, fleet tactics, environmental manipulation. Scale: Entire battlefield.
  4. Legendary (Levels 18-20): The Admiral Becomes the First Captain. Maritime sovereignty, campaign-scale influence, world map changes. Scale: Regions/Factions/Campaigns. (Note: Permanent world-changing effects require time, resources, and narrative consequences).

Chapter 5b: Least Captain’s Techniques (Levels 1-5)

These techniques establish the Wayfinder’s earliest identity. They emphasize positioning, environment, precision, and skill over raw statistical bonuses.

Seamanship Techniques

1. Sea Legs (Passive, Self): Ignore movement penalties from pitching decks, slick surfaces, rigging. Stand from prone as a swift action on a vessel. 2. Chart the Unknown (Standard, Area within 1 mile): Study terrain/currents for 1 min. Create a mental chart. Learn safe routes, unexplored regions, or if a route is artificial. (Reveals geography only). 3. Favor the Current (Swift, Self, 1 round): Ignore difficult terrain caused by water, mud, sand, snow, or loose stone for 1 round. 4. Mooring Line (Move, 30 ft.): Secure a rope/chain expertly. Allies crossing ignore the first Balance/Climb check. 5. Read the Wind (Standard, Self): Learn local weather trends, wind direction, and if dangerous natural weather is likely in the next few hours. 6. Shorefinder (Passive, Self): Always know the direction of the nearest coastline, river outlet, harbor, or navigable shoreline while outdoors.

Duelist Techniques

7. Opening Measure (Swift, 1 melee opponent, 1 round): If your next melee attack hits them this turn, you may take a 5-ft step that doesn’t count against normal movement. 8. Crossed Steel (Immediate, Self): When an adjacent opponent misses you, draw a weapon, switch grips, or reposition weapon without provoking attacks of opportunity. 9. Press the Guard (Standard, 1 opponent): Hit with an attack to deny the target a 5-ft step before the start of your next turn. 10. Low Feint (Swift, 1 opponent within 30 ft): The first ally to attack the target before your next turn gains a circumstance advantage (distraction/false opening). 11. Recover Balance (Immediate, Self): Reduce involuntary movement by 5 ft, or remain standing if forced movement was 5 ft or less. 12. Captain’s Challenge (Swift, 1 opponent within 30 ft, 1 round): If they move away, they provoke from you. If they attack an ally instead of you, you gain +2 on your next attack against them.

Corsair Techniques

13. First Over the Rail (Move, Self): Move normal speed without provoking AoO from the first enemy you pass when boarding/climbing. 14. Break the Line (Standard, Adjacent enemy): Make melee attack. If successful, target must give ground 5 ft or lose AoO until start of your next turn (Will negates). 15. Claim the Prize (Swift, 1 enemy damaged this turn): First ally to attack target can move 5 ft before or after without provoking AoO. 16. Boarding Hook (Move, 30 ft.): Pull yourself to a fixed point or pull an unattended object (up to heavy load limit) toward you. 17. Find the Weak Board (Standard, Object/Structure within 30 ft): Identify weak point. First attack within 10 mins ignores 2 hardness, reduces repair DC, or creates an opening. (Uses/day = Cha mod). 18. False Surrender (Standard, 1 creature within 30 ft): Bluff vs Sense Motive. Target gets -2 on next attack vs you and cannot make AoO against you until your next turn.

Fleet Techniques

19. Captain’s Signal (Swift, 1 ally within 30 ft): Ally can immediately take a 5-ft step. 20. Tight Formation (Standard, Allies within 10 ft): Allies can delay actions until immediately after another affected ally acts. Lasts 1 round. 21. Crew Rotation (Immediate, 2 willing allies within 30 ft): Swap two allies’ positions if attacked or forced to move. Doesn’t provoke AoO, max 5 ft. 22. Cover the Retreat (Immediate, 1 ally within 30 ft): When ally moves away from enemy, grant 5-ft bonus movement that doesn’t provoke AoO. 23. Shared Burden (Standard, 1 ally within 30 ft): Reduce forced movement on an ally by 5 ft for 1 round. 24. Quartermaster’s Eye (Standard, Vessel/Camp in sight): Inspect for 1 min. Accurately estimate food, water, ammo, common goods, shortages within 10% accuracy.

Hunter Techniques

25. Saltmark Trail (Standard, Touched creature/object, 24 hr): Place invisible tracking mark. Sense direction within 1 mile (Will negates). 26. Read the Wake (Standard, 30 ft area): Determine recent movement, numbers, size, direction, and rough timeframe of passage. 27. Hidden Anchorage (Passive, Self): Recognize natural concealment for ships/camps, ambush locations, and concealed approaches. 28. Ambush Route (Standard, 60 ft area): Determine optimal approach/escape/engagement point. Allies gain +2 on first Stealth/Survival/Initiative check related to it (lasts 1 hr). 29. Expedition Sense (Passive/Special, Self): +2 on Survival to identify natural hazards/poison. Observe 1 min to auto-detect obvious mundane hazards.

Mystic Techniques

31. Omen of the Tide (Standard, Self): Ask a simple question about a voyage/decision. Receive a symbolic omen indicating favorable/unfavorable conditions. 32. Whispering Compass (Standard, Self, 1 hr): Always know direction of a known destination/landmark you’ve visited. 33. Storm Listener (Passive, Self): Sense approaching natural storms, abnormal atmospheric patterns, or supernatural weather (but not its source). 34. First Captain Techniques

37. Captain’s Reputation (Passive, Self): Gain a competence bonus equal to half Wayfinder level on Diplomacy and Knowledge (local) in maritime communities. 38. Chapter 6: Campaign Engines

The campaign loop is: Explore → Discover → Record → Understand → Upgrade → Explore Further. Combat feeds discovery, and discovery changes the world.

The Discovery System & Captain’s Log

A Discovery is a verified, meaningful first-time revelation that expands the world. A rumor is not a discovery; it must be physically mapped, witnessed, or magically confirmed. - Wayfinding Lore: Accumulated understanding. Never spent, only gained. Used to unlock Fusion Techniques, ship awakenings, and legendary status. - Discovery Points (DP): Short-term resource spent on Fusion Techniques and special class abilities (max 3-10, refreshes during downtime). - The Captain’s Log: A magical, permanent record. It tracks discoveries across categories (Charts, Species, Trade, Magic, Spirits, History, Ships). The Log contains “Hidden Tags” (e.g., Arcane, Drow, First Captain) tracked by the DM that unlock future transformations or ship upgrades.

Discovery Tiers

  1. Local: Small area (e.g., hidden cove). Grants 1 Lore, normal XP.
  2. Regional: Changes travel/trade for a region (e.g., safe shipping lane). Grants 2 Lore, ship/port benefit.
  3. Major: Matters to a major faction (e.g., lost civilization outpost). Grants 4 Lore, strong faction reaction, unlock.
  4. World-Shaping: Changes the map or economy (e.g., Underdark ocean route). Grants 8 Lore, major political shift.
  5. Mythic: Happens only in legend (e.g., First Captain revelation). Grants 12 Lore, capstone narrative effects.

The Passage System

The ocean is the dungeon. Passages are the “rooms.” A voyage consists of multiple Passages. 1. Captain’s Orders: Set the priority (Fast, Survey, Hunt, Hidden, Trade, Deep). 2. The Sea Responds: DM determines weather, encounters, ruins, or monsters. 3. The Crew Acts: Navigators, lookouts, and specialists report. 4. Player Decisions: Fight, negotiate, hide, trade, board. 5. Resolution: Update supplies, repairs, Lore, and morale.

Boarding Combat (The Corsair Dungeon)

A hostile ship is a moving dungeon. Boarding has phases: Close distance, Cannon exchange, Hook, Swing across, Deck battle, Officer duel, Capture, Salvage.

Ship Construction Rules Engine

A ship is built using Ship Construction Points (SCP). - Size 1 (Small Craft): 50 SCP, 1 Hull slot. (Scout/Smuggler). - Size 2 (Light Vessel): 100 SCP, 2 Hull slots. (Piracy/Fast attack). - Size 3 (Medium Vessel): 200 SCP, 3 Hull slots. (Professional pirate ship). - Size 4 (Heavy Vessel): 350 SCP, 5 Hull slots. (Frigate/Galleon). - Size 5 (Capital Vessel): 600 SCP, 8 Hull slots. (Man-o-war).

Categories & Materials: SCP is spent on Structure, Propulsion, Combat, Utility, and Special Systems. Weapons take up slots (e.g., Light Cannon = 1 slot, 5 SCP; Dragonfire Cannon = 5 slots, 75 SCP). Materials modify Hull Strength, Weight, and Magical Affinity. (e.g., Ironwood adds +40% Hull, +20% Weight; Darkwood adds +15% Hull, -30% Weight).

Ships can install Magical (teleportation anchors), Psionic (thought navigation), Divine (celestial sails), or Spirit (living figureheads) systems.

Chapter 7: Dungeon Master’s Guide

A Wayfinder campaign should feel like routes opening, rivals responding, nations adjusting, and legends forming. The DM’s role is not to restrict the Wayfinder’s influence, but to ensure that influence creates new challenges and opportunities.

Running the Campaign

  • Naval Campaigns: Track positioning, morale, supplies, and weather. Captain’s Techniques create tactical advantages, not automatic victory.
  • Exploration Campaigns: Exploration techniques should reveal clues and paths, but they do not eliminate the discovery process. Discovering a new route creates story (e.g., rival explorers, ancient guardians).
  • Political Campaigns: First Captain techniques create influence and reputation, not mind control. The captain can gain audiences or secure trade agreements, but kings do not automatically surrender their thrones.
  • Fleet Warfare: Fleet conflicts are strategic encounters. Ask: Who has superior intelligence? Who controls positioning? What are the objectives?

Handling Consequences

Every major achievement creates reactions. - Immediate Effect: What changes now? - Local Response: Who notices? (e.g., local pirates, merchants). - Regional Response: Who reacts? (e.g., Crimson Dominion investigates, Drow launch a raid). - Long-Term Consequence: How does the world change? (e.g., a new era of exploration begins).

Tracking the World

  • Captain Reputation: Fame, fear, and political influence.
  • Maritime Relations: Allies, enemies, and neutral factions.
  • Known Routes: Safe paths, dangers, and discoveries.

Exploit Prevention & Rules Clarifications

To preserve the campaign sandbox and prevent the Wayfinder from bypassing all gameplay:

  1. Fleet + First Captain Interaction:
    • Rule: Fleet techniques affect tactical command during active operations. First Captain techniques affect strategic influence. A creature can benefit from only one Fleet technique command effect at a time.
  2. Mystic/Hunter Information Limits:
    • Rule: Information gained through techniques provides direction, clues, and probabilities. It does not automatically reveal all hidden creatures, secret motives, or bypass investigation. Perfect information destroys mystery.
  3. Corsair Siege Limits:
    • Rule: Siege-related techniques create openings and tactical advantages. They do not automatically destroy structures, ships, or magical defenses. You create a breach; the fortress does not instantly collapse.
  4. Legendary World Effects:
    • Rule: Permanent world-changing effects (e.g., discovering impossible routes, establishing sovereign domains) require time, resources, and narrative consequences. The captain can influence history, but they do not own it.
  5. Passive & Action Economy Limits:
    • Rule: Bonuses from Captain’s Techniques follow normal D&D 3.5 stacking rules. A Wayfinder may use only one Immediate-Action Captain’s Technique per round.

Final DM Principle

The captain may change the world, but the world always responds. A Wayfinder’s greatest victories should not close the campaign; they should create the next chapter.

Introduction: Running a Captain’s Campaign

Captain’s Techniques are designed around a different style of advancement than traditional class features.

A traditional adventurer asks:

“How do I overcome this challenge?”

A Wayfinder captain asks:

“How does overcoming this challenge change the voyage?”

Captain’s Techniques are intended to influence:

naval campaigns,

exploration campaigns,

pirate conflicts,

political campaigns,

fleet warfare,

island kingdoms,

supernatural oceans.

The DM’s role is not to limit Captain’s Techniques. The role is to allow those abilities to create meaningful consequences.

A successful Captain’s Techniques campaign should show:

routes opening,

rivals adapting,

factions responding,

territories changing,

legends forming.

The subsystem’s core philosophy is that power creates opportunities and consequences rather than removing the need for adventure. ChatGPT-Pirate Adventure Setup 2.txt

Section 1: Managing Captain Abilities

1.1 Evaluating Powerful Abilities

Captain’s Techniques differ from ordinary abilities because many affect the world around the character.

A DM should evaluate abilities using three questions:

  1. What problem does the ability solve?

Examples:

A Seamanship technique may solve:

dangerous navigation,

difficult travel,

environmental hazards.

A Fleet technique may solve:

coordination problems,

command issues,

battlefield organization.

A First Captain technique may solve:

organizational limitations,

diplomatic barriers,

logistical challenges.

The question is not:

“Does this ability bypass the adventure?”

The question is:

“What new situation does this ability create?”

1.2 Maintaining Player Agency

Captain’s Techniques should increase player agency.

They should allow players to:

choose routes,

influence factions,

command resources,

establish reputations,

change strategic situations.

They should not remove:

meaningful choices,

consequences,

opposing interests,

campaign uncertainty.

Example:

A Mystic Navigator discovers a hidden sea route.

Incorrect interpretation:

“The party automatically wins every voyage.”

Correct interpretation:

“The party gains access to a route others cannot use. Now merchants, pirates, kingdoms, and ancient guardians care about that route.”

The ability creates story pressure.

It does not end the story.

1.3 Avoiding Removed Choices

The primary DM warning:

Do not convert influence into certainty.

Captain’s Techniques should not become:

automatic diplomacy,

automatic victory,

perfect information,

unlimited resources.

The subsystem balance audit specifically identifies this concern:

Fleet should affect battle, not replace military campaigns.

First Captain should affect organizations, not create automatic obedience.

Mystic and Hunter should provide information, not absolute knowledge.

Corsair should create openings, not automatic destruction. ChatGPT-Pirate Adventure Setup 2.txt
