# Chapter 20: Regional Gazetteer - The Grand Merchant Consortium

*“We do not conquer islands, Captain. We purchase them. It is infinitely cheaper than war, and the locals even thank us for it.”* — High Factor Vance of the Gold Scale

The Grand Merchant Consortium is the economic heart of the Shattered Expanse. They are not a traditional nation with borders, but rather a hyper-capitalist coalition of trading companies, banking guilds, and mercenary navies. They believe that gold is the only true magic in the world, and they possess enough of it to buy or bury any threat.

## 1. Geography, Demographics & Hubs

The Consortium's power does not lie in a contiguous landmass, but in their absolute control over the Great Magical Trade Network.

**Population Overview:**
- Total Estimated Population: ~1.2 Million (Direct employees and citizens)
- The High Factors (Ruling Board): <1%
- Guild Members & Contracted Sailors: 40%
- Indentured Laborers & Debtors: 59%

### Port Meridian (Capital Hub)
Port Meridian is a massive, sprawling city-state built across a central, highly strategic archipelago. It is the wealthiest location in the known world. The city is a dizzying array of toll bridges, towering marble banks, and crowded, neon-lit arcane bazaars. The Consortium doesn't "own" the city in a royal sense—they just own the deeds to every single building and ship inside it.
- **The Golden Spire:** The central banking and administration hub where the Board of Factors meets.
- **The Silk Wards:** Opulent districts for high-ranking merchants and visiting diplomats.
- **The Debt Docks:** Slums where those who have defaulted on Consortium loans are forced to work off their debt in the shipyards.

### The Waystations
The Consortium maintains dozens of heavily fortified, neutral trading posts scattered across the Charted Waters. These serve as safe havens, resupply points, and banking outposts.

## 2. Magic Systems Integration

The Consortium views magic purely as a commodity and a logistical tool.

- **Arcane Magic (Logistics & Profit):** The primary magic used by the Consortium. They employ wizards not as soldiers, but as navigators, weather-controllers, and accountants. Illusion magic is heavily regulated to prevent counterfeiting.
- **Psionics (Contracts & Security):** Psions are highly paid to act as living polygraphs during negotiations and to secure vaults against magical intrusion.
- **Spirit Magic (Commodified):** They don't revere spirits; they commodify them. They harvest rare pearls, coral, and leviathan bones to sell to Arcane researchers at a massive markup.
- **Deep Magic (The Black Market):** The Consortium publicly outlaws Deep Magic, but secretly, wealthy Factors pay exorbitant sums for Drow artifacts pulled from the Sunless Sea.

## 3. Faction Relations (Geopolitical Matrix)

The Consortium’s foreign policy is simple: maximize profit, minimize risk.

- **Free Captains Alliance:** *Profitable Nuisance.* They publicly condemn piracy and execute pirates to maintain a "safe" image, but secretly hire Free Captains as deniable privateers to sabotage rival trade routes.
- **Crimson Dominion:** *Cold War / Essential Client.* The Dominion is the Consortium's biggest customer for raw materials. The Consortium walks a razor's edge, selling them enough to profit, but not enough to let the Dominion become completely self-sufficient and annex Port Meridian.
- **Drowned Silk Houses (Drow):** *Black Market Partners.* The Consortium officially denies trading with the Drow, while simultaneously maintaining highly lucrative smuggling routes with them through the Drown-Gates.
- **Spirit Keepers:** *Annoying Obstacles.* The Keepers constantly blockade Consortium whaling and logging operations. The Consortium views them as backward zealots holding up progress.
- **Independent Salvagers:** *Primary Investors.* The Consortium funds Salvager expeditions into the Unwritten Sea, retaining exclusive rights to auction off any First Captain technology they find.

## 4. Societal Hierarchy (The Corporate Ladder)

Status in the Consortium is entirely determined by wealth and guild rank.

1. **The Board of Factors:** The CEOs of the massive trading companies. They dictate economic policy, interest rates, and naval deployment.
2. **The Guildmasters:** Heads of specific industries (e.g., The Spice Cartel, The Arcane Materials Guild). 
3. **The Gold Cloaks:** The elite, highly paid mercenary army and private navy that enforces Consortium contracts and protects the trade routes.
4. **The Clerks & Sailors:** The middle class. Bureaucrats, accountants, and contracted sailors who keep the gears of commerce turning.
5. **The Indentured:** Those who fell into debt. They are not technically "slaves" like in the Dominion; they are "contractually obligated laborers." The debt is often mathematically impossible to pay off.

## 5. Internal Politics & Rival Factions

The Consortium is a cutthroat meritocracy where hostile takeovers are literal.

- **The Expansionists:** Factors who believe the Consortium should build a massive military to actively conquer the Unwritten Sea and monopolize First Captain ruins.
- **The Shadow Brokers:** Guilds deeply invested in the black market, wanting to formalize trade with the Drow and the pirate lords.
- **The Debtors' Union:** A growing, violent underground movement of indentured laborers plotting to burn the central bank ledgers to free millions from generational debt.
- **Corporate Sabotage:** Factors routinely hire assassins, privateers, and spies to ruin their rivals' trade routes and stock prices. 

## 6. The Military Machine

The Consortium's military is entirely privatized. They do not have citizens defending a homeland; they have mercenaries defending cargo.

- **Naval Fleet Composition:** 
  - *Treasure Galleons:* Massive, heavily warded floating vaults designed to transport fortunes. They are slow but practically unsinkable.
  - *The Gold Cloak Escorts:* Sleek, fast, heavily armed frigates that guard the trade lanes.
- **Shipboard Weaponry:** They prioritize defense. Their ships are equipped with advanced Abjuration shielding, enchanted sails for speed, and magical flares to summon nearby mercenary fleets.
- **Combat Tactics:** They prefer to buy off enemies. If forced into a fight, they rely on overwhelming, expensive firepower (e.g., mercenaries armed with wands and enchanted ammunition) and rapid tactical retreats to protect their investments.
