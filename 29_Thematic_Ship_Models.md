# Chapter 29: Thematic Ship Models

While Chapter 28 provides the mechanical statblocks for enemy vessels, this chapter provides the "theater of the mind." These thematic models describe what the ships look like, what they smell like, how they are laid out internally, and how the crew behaves in combat. DMs can use these descriptions to bring the Shattered Expanse to life.

---

## 1. The Crimson Dominion

Dominion ships are industrial nightmares of iron and smoke, designed to project absolute authority.

### The Ironclad Escort (Size 2)
*The fist of the Dominion, patrolling borders and enforcing blockades.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A low, squat vessel almost entirely encased in riveted, dark-grey Aether-Iron. It lacks traditional sails, driven instead by a churning rear paddlewheel powered by an arcane furnace. A thick plume of black, acrid smoke constantly trails behind it.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The deck is cramped, hot, and smells strongly of ozone and sulfur. The command deck is completely enclosed in an iron pillbox to protect the Captain. 
- **Encounter Hook:** The players are caught sailing through Dominion-claimed waters. The Ironclad does not ask for identification; it fires a warning shot of pure arcane energy across the bow and demands immediate surrender and boarding for "inspection."
- **Tactical Behavior:** Relentless pursuit. They will use their *Arcane Ramming* ability to smash through smaller escort vessels or debris to get directly alongside their target.

### The Magi-Cruiser (Size 3)
*A line-breaker designed to deliver devastating magical payloads.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A sharp, angular vessel that cuts through the waves like a spearhead. It features three massive, glowing arcane conduits (acting as masts) that hum with contained elemental energy. The hull is painted a deep, blood red.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** Impeccably clean and highly disciplined. The lower decks feature heavily warded containment rooms where chained, exhausted spellcasters (often slaves) act as living batteries for the ship's Precursor Cannon.
- **Encounter Hook:** A Magi-Cruiser is deployed specifically to hunt down the players after they successfully raid a Dominion stronghold. 
- **Tactical Behavior:** Methodical artillery bombardment. The Captain will keep the ship at maximum range, relying on the Bosun's *Aether Surge* to pummel the enemy ship into submission before ever attempting a boarding action.

### The Dreadnought (Size 4)
*A floating fortress. Terrifyingly durable, but incredibly cumbersome.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A moving mountain of iron. It is so heavy it sits dangerously low in the water. It bristles with multi-tiered cannon batteries, and its central smokestacks are tall enough to rival a small castle tower.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The interior is a labyrinth of blistering hot boiler rooms, barracks for hundreds of marines, and lavish, insulated officer's quarters. The holding cells in the brig are specifically designed to suppress magic.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players must break a Dominion siege on a Free Captain harbor, and the Dreadnought is the anchor of the blockade.
- **Tactical Behavior:** The Dreadnought moves slowly, establishing an *Unbreakable Line*. It acts as a mobile command center, absorbing fire while its massive broadsides methodically tear apart the largest threats on the battlefield.

## 2. The Grand Merchant Consortium

Consortium ships are designed to protect investments. They are ostentatious, heavily shielded, and prioritize cargo over crew comfort.

### The Audit-Frigate (Size 2)
*Fast, shielded escorts designed to stall enemies while cargo escapes.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** Sleek and aerodynamic, painted in pristine white and gold. The hull is covered in subtle, glowing blue geometric runes (the Abjuration Shield). 
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** Functional but highly polished. The captain's cabin resembles a wealthy banker's office, complete with mahogany desks and magical ledgers. The air smells of expensive sea-salt perfume and polished brass.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players are hired to protect a Salvager vessel, and an Audit-Frigate intercepts them, demanding the "Guild Tax" on any recovered artifacts.
- **Tactical Behavior:** Annoy and delay. The Captain will use the *Bribe* action to confuse the enemy, while the ship uses its speed to stay out of ramming range, constantly regenerating its shield.

### The Dividend-Galleon (Size 3)
*A heavy cargo hauler that relies entirely on magical defenses to survive.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A fat, wide-bodied galleon with massive, billowing yellow sails. The ship sits heavily in the water, visibly laden with cargo. The Abjuration Shield is so thick it visibly ripples the air around the hull.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The entire lower deck is a massive *Smuggler's Hold* Extra-Dimensional space. The crew quarters are incredibly cramped to maximize profit margins. The Ledger-Mage sits in a suspended glass dome above the main deck to calculate combat risks.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players discover a distressed Dividend-Galleon that has been caught in a storm, its shields fluctuating. Do they help it for a reward, or pirate its massive cargo?
- **Tactical Behavior:** Evasive cowardice. The ship will drop an *Alchemical Smokescreen* at the first sign of real danger and attempt to flee. If cornered, the Bosun will constantly *Divert Power* to keep the shields up until reinforcements arrive.

### The Vault-Ship (Size 4)
*An unsinkable, slow-moving bank vault transporting immense wealth.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A colossal, square-rigged behemoth. It looks less like a ship and more like a floating fortress of polished stone and gilded wood. The hull is reinforced with sheets of alchemical lead to block scrying.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The interior is literally a bank vault. The cargo is kept behind massive, puzzle-locked iron doors. The Gold Cloak Enforcers patrol the decks in perfectly synchronized shifts.
- **Encounter Hook:** The ultimate heist. The players have been hired by the Free Captains to intercept a Vault-Ship carrying a year's worth of collected taxes.
- **Tactical Behavior:** *Total Lockdown.* The Vault-Ship will stop moving entirely, doubling its shield regeneration, and rely on its massive array of broadside cannons and Siege-Breaker Mortars to obliterate anything that gets too close.

## 3. The Drowned Silk Houses

Drow ships are terrifying, biological atrocities that strike from the pitch-black depths.

### The Viper-Skiff (Size 2)
*A stealthy, silent assassin sub.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** Looks like a massive, 60-foot-long sleek black eel. It has no sails, no deck, and no windows. It moves silently beneath the waves, propelled by undulating, magically animated fins.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** There is no "deck." The interior is a cramped, suffocating tube of ribbed bone and pulsing purple flesh. The air is stale, humid, and smells of rotting fish. The crew operates in complete darkness using darkvision.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players are transporting a high-value political prisoner (or defector). A Viper-Skiff tracks them and attempts an underwater assassination.
- **Tactical Behavior:** *Silent Running.* The Skiff will stay submerged, tracking the target using sonar. It will surface directly alongside the target in the dead of night, deploy assassins, and submerge again before the alarm is raised.

### The Arachna-Submersible (Size 3)
*A terrifying hunter made of enchanted chitin.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** The hollowed-out exoskeleton of a massive deep-sea crab or spider. It bristles with spiked legs that it uses to physically cling to the underside of enemy ships. 
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The "command bridge" is located in the transparent skull-casing of the beast. The Flesh-Weavers constantly stitch the internal membranes together to keep the water out. The ship literally bleeds when damaged.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players' ship suddenly stops moving in open water. An Arachna-Submersible has clamped onto their keel from below and is actively freezing the water around them with Cryo-Torpedoes.
- **Tactical Behavior:** The *Abyssal Breach*. The ship will erupt from the water, creating massive waves, and immediately initiate a *Deployment* of Crimson Guard marines directly onto the enemy deck under the cover of magical *Darkness*.

### The Necropolis-Carrier (Size 4)
*A mobile hive built into a skeletal whale.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A 300-foot-long undead leviathan. Its ribcage is exposed, and within the cavernous chest cavity, glowing green necromantic fires illuminate thousands of swarming thralls. 
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** A nightmare of undeath. The decks are made of calcified bone. The ship is commanded by a Matron-Admiral who sits on a throne of coral attached directly to the leviathan's exposed spine, controlling it telepathically.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players must delve into the Sunless Sea, and their path is blocked by a Necropolis-Carrier guarding a Drown-Gate.
- **Tactical Behavior:** Overwhelming numbers. The Carrier will release a *Thrall Swarm* of undead Kuo-toa to clog the water and foul enemy propellers/rudders, while unleashing devastating barrages of Sonic Projectors that shatter enemy hulls from the inside out.

## 4. The Free Captains Alliance

Alliance ships are highly customized, wildly dangerous, and built for speed and shock-and-awe.

### The Zephyr-Sloop (Size 2)
*Impossibly fast blockade runners built for smuggling.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A tiny hull with an absurd amount of rigging and *Scavenger Sails*. The sails are a patchwork of different colored canvas, kraken hide, and elemental silk. The ship looks like it should capsize in a stiff breeze.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** Chaotic and cluttered. Ropes are everywhere. The deck smells of cheap rum, sea spray, and black powder. The ship is stripped of all luxury to reduce weight.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players need to hire a smuggler to get them past a Dominion blockade, and a cocky Wayfinder captain offers them a ride on a Zephyr-Sloop.
- **Tactical Behavior:** *Full Sail!* They will never engage in a prolonged fight. They rely on their ridiculous speed and maneuverability to dart through narrow reefs where heavier ships cannot follow, taking potshots with Swivel Guns.

### The Corsair-Brig (Size 3)
*The backbone of the pirate fleets, built for swift boarding.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A menacing, dark-wood brigantine. It is bristling with more cannons than its size category should normally allow. The prow is often decorated with a terrifying (and sometimes living) figurehead.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** Loud, rowdy, and dangerous. The Powder Monkeys run frantically between decks. Weapons are piled everywhere. The Quartermaster can usually be found shouting orders from the main mast.
- **Encounter Hook:** A classic pirate ambush. The players are caught in a magically summoned fog bank, only to hear sea-shanties echoing through the mist just before the Corsair-Brig rams them.
- **Tactical Behavior:** Aggressive and suicidal. They rely on *Jury-Rigged Volatility* for massive burst damage to cripple the enemy, followed immediately by a *Grapple and Board!* action to overwhelm the defenders in melee.

### The Kraken-Class Flagship (Size 4)
*A stolen, heavily modified capital ship prioritizing guns over armor.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A massive Dominion Dreadnought or Consortium Galleon that has been aggressively "redecorated." The armor has been stripped away to increase speed, replaced by terrifying war-paint, spiked rams, and scavenged Precursor weaponry that sparks with unstable energy.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** It feels less like a ship and more like a floating, heavily armed tavern. The Pirate Lord commands from a throne made of stolen Dominion armor plates. 
- **Encounter Hook:** The players have angered the Brethren Court, and Pirate King Jax has sent his personal Flagship to make an example of them.
- **Tactical Behavior:** Pure, overwhelming force. The Pirate Lord will issue the *Pirate King's Command*, sending hundreds of screaming corsairs swinging across the rigging while the unstable Precursor Cannons tear the enemy hull to shreds.

## 5. The Spirit Keepers

Keeper ships are organic, symbiotic entities that use the ocean itself as a weapon.

### The Bramble-Skiff (Size 2)
*A stealthy, organic scout vessel.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** It doesn't look like a ship; it looks like a floating tangle of driftwood, mangrove roots, and green kelp. It has no visible sails, moving instead by the manipulation of water currents beneath it.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The deck is soft and spongy, made of living moss and wet wood. The air smells strongly of fresh rain and blooming lotus. The crew moves silently, communicating through hand signs and spirit whispers.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players anchor near a seemingly deserted island, only to realize the "mangrove swamp" next to them is actually a fleet of Bramble-Skiffs surrounding them.
- **Tactical Behavior:** *Camouflage.* They rely on ambush and guerrilla tactics. They will decloak, fire a volley of Coral-Shard Ballistae, and immediately blend back into the coastal foliage.

### The Iron-Reef Galleon (Size 3)
*A heavy, calcified combat vessel.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A sunken ship that has been brought back to the surface, completely encrusted in magically hardened, razor-sharp coral. The *Living Iron-Wood* hull is jagged and vibrant with bioluminescence. 
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** The interior is cavernous and damp, illuminated by glowing algae. Water flows freely through the lower decks, allowing bonded aquatic elementals to swim through the ship. 
- **Encounter Hook:** A Keeper Warden intercepts the players' ship because they are carrying a corrupted artifact that is polluting the Aether Streams.
- **Tactical Behavior:** Area denial. The First Mate will use *Tide-Caller's Wind* to stall the enemy ship, while the Shamanic Focuses call down lightning strikes. If rammed, their *Grasping Vines* will trap the enemy vessel against the razor-sharp coral hull.

### The World-Tree Carrier (Size 4)
*A massive floating island carrying enraged sea beasts.*
- **Visual Silhouette:** A literal floating island. A massive, ancient banyan tree grows from the center of a calcified coral atoll. The roots dangle hundreds of feet into the ocean. The canopy acts as a massive sail catching the Aether winds.
- **Deck & Aesthetics:** A thriving, enclosed ecosystem. Birds nest in the rigging, and freshwater springs bubble up from the deck. The High Elder communes with spirits at the base of the central tree.
- **Encounter Hook:** The players must seek out a World-Tree Carrier to ask a High Elder how to cure a magical plague affecting their crew.
- **Tactical Behavior:** The Wrath of Nature. The Carrier rarely fires its own weapons. Instead, it will *Deploy Beasts*, unleashing bonded Dire Sharks, Water Elementals, and Giant Squids to tear the enemy fleet apart while the High Elder manipulates the ocean currents to crush the enemy against the reefs.
